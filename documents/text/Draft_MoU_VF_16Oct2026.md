## PREAMBLE
**WHEREAS,**
- The Directorate of Horticulture functions under the Department of Agriculture, Government of Bihar, and is responsible for planning and implementing horticulture development programmes in the State, including programmes relating to fruits, vegetables, flowers, medicinal & aromatic plants, spices, makhana (fox nut) and other horticulture & plantation crops. The Department is also responsible for creating horticulture infrastructure including nurseries, protected cultivation structures, tissue culture units and mushroom units, together with post-harvest management (PHM) facilities such as primary processing units, pack houses, pre-cooling chambers, ripening chambers, cold storages, storage units and reefer vans;
- Quality Council of India was set up through a decision of the Union Cabinet with the mandate of establishing and operating the national accreditation structure and promoting quality across the country. QCI delivers its mandate through its constituent Boards and Divisions, which together offer accreditation, quality scheme development, project planning and implementation, training and capacity building, quality promotion and industry excellence services; and
- Horticulture is a major source of livelihood and income diversification for the farmers of Bihar, and the horticulture crops like litchi, makhana, banana, mango and flowers, are increasingly directed at distant domestic and export markets where buyers expect consistent grade, safety and verifiable provenance.

**NOW, THEREFORE,** the Parties have agreed to enter this MoU to give effect to the foregoing and, in recognition of their shared interests and objectives, record their mutual understanding as follows:

## ARTICLE - 1: OBJECTIVES
This MoU is entered into with the following objectives:
- To plan, design and implement structured, time-bound interventions that build a quality and conformity assessment ecosystem for the horticulture value chains of Bihar, covering both backward and forward linkage part of the crops; including grading, processing, testing, traceability etc.
- To strengthen high-quality testing capacity within the horticulture clusters of the State and the technical competence of the workforce across the value chain, so that quality created in Bihar can be demonstrated, verified and recognised in domestic and export markets
- To support better price realisation for cultivators, processors and Farmer Producer Organisations (FPOs), and improved export readiness of the sector, through credible quality assurance.
- To institutionalise quality governance, monitoring and periodic review within State institutions.
- To align the quality interventions with the horticulture schemes/programs and infrastructure supported by the State and Centre. so that quality assurance is built around the horticulture ecosystem

## ARTICLE - 2: SCOPE OF COLLABORATION
The key technical offerings by both the Parties shall include, but are not limited to, the following areas of collaboration:

**Programme Management and Governance**
- Providing Programme Management Unit (PMU) services, as and when required by the Directorate, to coordinate the interventions undertaken under this MoU and act as the single point of accountability for delivery. Where the PMU is engaged, its services may include:
  - preparation of an integrated baseline and diagnostic plan and a consolidated workplan;
  - operation of a central Management Information System (MIS) and quality dashboard; and
  - submission of monthly and quarterly progress reports, or such other reports as may be required by DoH from time to time;
- Supporting the formation and institutionalisation of a Horticulture Quality Cell within the Directorate, with QCI mentorship.

**Technical Expertise, Advisory and Baseline Studies**
- Providing technical expertise and advisory support to the Directorate for planning and implementing quality interventions across the backward and forward linkages of horticulture value chains.
- Baseline and diagnostic studies, including characterisation of prevailing grade classes (wherever applicable), gap assessment and readiness studies and audit of a representative sample of identified crop, and of existing and sanctioned laboratory facilities.
- Collection and analysis of field data and samples from production and processing clusters (such as the litchi, makhana, mango growing areas of Bihar), and sharing of findings and insights to inform standards, interventions and policy decisions.

**Standards, Specifications and Process Development**
- Development of measurable grading specifications for priority horticulture produce (such as size, popping quality, moisture and defect parameters for makhana).
- Development of crop-specific post-harvest handling and process standards, including Good Hygiene Practices (GHP), Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP) and Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Point (HACCP) requirements, in a tiered compliance model suited to units of different scales.
- Development of a calibration and verification protocol for grading and sorting equipment used at cluster and FPO facilities.

**Laboratory Infrastructure and Accreditation**
- Laboratory needs assessment and network design for the horticulture clusters of the State, including the testing requirements of the crops both for domestic and global market.
- Definition of the scope of accreditation for each laboratory and design-stage technical assistance for laboratories under creation.
- Accreditation support to laboratories under the Directorate of Horticulture, Department of Agriculture, Bihar including training, documentation, method validation, proficiency testing linkage, pre-assessment, handholding and post-accreditation support, method validation, proficiency testing linkage, pre-assessment and post-accreditation support.

**Certification, Conformity Assessment and Market Surveillance**
- Design and operationalisation of a product project certification scheme and quality mark for horticulture produce, including a GI authentication and anti-adulteration protocol for GI-registered produce such as Mithila Makhana, Shahi Litchi, Bhagalpuri Zardalu Mango and Magahi Paan.
- Support in empanelment and Training, handholding and capacity building of conformity assessment bodies.
- Cluster-mode Zero Defect Zero Effect (ZED) certification of FPOs, processing and packaging units (such as makhana FPOs and other processing units), with incentive convergence and progression tracking.
- Design and operationalisation of a market surveillance and inspection framework, including masked and random sampling protocols for the collection and analysis of samples of horticulture produce, a periodic quality surveillance report, and a grievance and non-conformity redressal mechanism.
- Design and institutionalisation of a recognition mechanism for quality and export excellence in the horticulture sector.

**Traceability, Export Readiness and Technological Interventions**
- Design and piloting of a traceability system for the horticulture export value chains with selected exporters operating from Bihar, compatible with existing national platforms.
- Evaluation of the export preparedness of exporters and FPOs.
- Piloting of innovative technologies and analytics to demonstrate their effectiveness in real-world conditions across the horticulture value chain.

**Training, Capacity Building and Stakeholder Engagement**
- Design of a tiered, multi-lingual training curriculum and a master trainer programme to build a resident pool of certified trainers within Bihar.
- Training and certification-linked skilling of inspectors, samplers, laboratory analysts and other technical personnel, with an annual refresher and reassessment cycle.
- Awareness and stakeholder engagement programmes, including seminars, workshops and conferences at State and district levels, for farmers, FPOs, processors, exporters and officials.

**Replication and Future Interventions**
- Replication of the standards, tools, training and institutional arrangements developed for Makhana under this MoU to other horticulture value chains of Bihar, such as litchi, banana, mango, flowers, mushroom and vegetables, as identified by the Directorate through different baseline studies and market research etc.
- Undertaking such other activities as may be mutually agreed between the Parties for achieving the objectives of this MoU.

The makhana value chain, being supported by the National Makhana Board, is proposed to be taken up as the first intervention under this MoU. The activities listed above will be designed, piloted and validated for makhana first and shall serve as the model for other horticulture value chains of the State, which may be taken up subsequently by mutual agreement of the Parties. The sequencing, timelines and deliverables for each activity shall be determined by the Joint Coordination Committee constituted under Article 4 and set out as per the respective projects or work orders

## ARTICLE - 3: FINANCIAL UNDERSTANDING
- This MoU outlines the key points of the present understanding of the Parties. Specific interventions will be undertaken as projects/initiatives with financial obligations, and each proposal based on the scope of work will be issued subject to the approval of the competent authority.
- The Parties may enter into definitive work orders/service agreements for pursuing such interventions on mutually agreed terms and conditions.
- The financial proposal from QCI with respect to the scope mentioned in Article 2 above will be submitted separately after the execution of this MoU.

## ARTICLE - 4: ADMINISTRATION OF THE MoU
For purposes of coordination and administration of this MoU, the Parties shall designate their respective contact/focal points within seven (7) working days of signing this MoU. The working arrangement shall be executed through a Joint Coordination Committee (JCC) comprising official representatives of both Parties to ensure smooth coordination of the programmes. The JCC shall be co-chaired by the designated representatives of the Parties, and each Party shall determine the composition of the JCC for its side.

The JCC shall determine the appropriate mode of engagement for each collaboration. These modes may include, but are not limited to:
- Project-based collaborations between QCI and DoH;
- Any other form of engagement as deemed appropriate and mutually beneficial, subject to the discretion and approval of the JCC.

## ARTICLE - 5: TERM OF MoU
This MoU shall be valid for three (3) years from the date of its execution and may be extended beyond its term by mutual written consent of the Parties. However, agreements concluded under this MoU shall remain valid until the concerned Parties have fully discharged their contractual obligations and responsibilities as agreed upon under individual work orders.

## ARTICLE - 6: INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY RIGHTS
The Parties recognise that any Intellectual Property owned or developed by a Party or by any agency acting on its behalf shall remain the sole and exclusive property of the developing Party, unless otherwise agreed through a separate written agreement.

## ARTICLE - 7: CONFIDENTIALITY
The Parties agree to maintain the confidentiality of documents and information exchanged during the implementation of this MoU. "Confidential Information" includes non-public, proprietary information and materials exchanged during the term of this MoU.

## ARTICLE - 8: INDEMNITY
Each Party agrees to defend, indemnify and hold the other Party and its employees, directors and officers harmless from any claims or losses arising from any negligent act or omission, any breach of its obligations under this MoU, or any violation of applicable law, rule or regulation by it or its agents, during and after the term of this MoU.

## ARTICLE - 9: AMENDMENT
Any amendment or waiver concerning any provision of this MoU shall be valid only if made in writing, signed by both Parties or their authorised representatives, and specifically stating that it is an amendment of this MoU. This MoU may be modified or renewed only with the written consent of both Parties. Any modification shall take effect from the date on which it is executed, unless otherwise agreed. No failure or delay in exercising any right, power or privilege hereunder shall operate as a waiver thereof, nor shall any single or partial exercise thereof preclude any other or further exercise thereof or the exercise of any other right, power or privilege hereunder.

## ARTICLE - 10: FORCE MAJEURE
Neither Party shall be responsible to the other for any failure or delay in the performance of its obligations under this MoU to the extent that such failure or delay is caused by Force Majeure or other contingencies beyond its reasonable control. Performance of any obligation affected by a Force Majeure event shall be resumed as soon as reasonably possible after the cessation of such event and its effects.

## ARTICLE - 11: FURTHER COOPERATION
This MoU is signed in good faith as an indication of the sincere intention of the Parties to build upon the mutual respect and confidence they have for each other, and the Parties shall carry out the activities under this MoU in good faith. The finer details regarding implementation shall be set out in the definitive agreements contemplated in Article 3.

## ARTICLE - 12: DISPUTE RESOLUTION
Any dispute, controversy or claim arising out of or concerning this MoU, or the breach, termination or invalidity thereof, shall be settled amicably by negotiation between the Parties.

## ARTICLE - 13: GOVERNING LAW
This MoU shall be governed by, and construed in accordance with, the prevailing laws of India, subject to the exclusive jurisdiction of the courts at Patna.

## ARTICLE - 14: MISCELLANEOUS
This MoU shall not be valid until approved and executed by authorised representatives of both Parties. Further, the signatories to this MoU represent and warrant that they have all the necessary authority and power to enter this MoU and perform its obligations hereunder.

## ARTICLE - 15: TERMINATION
During the term of this MoU, either Party may terminate it, whether for breach of any of its terms and conditions or otherwise, by giving one (1) month's written notice to the other Party. Failure of either Party to terminate this MoU on account of breach or default by the other shall not constitute a waiver of that Party's right to terminate it.

In the event of termination, the rights and obligations of the Parties shall be settled by mutual discussion; the financial statement shall take into consideration not only the expenditure incurred but also the expenditure committed by the Parties, as per the audited statement.

Upon termination of this MoU for any reason, the Parties shall work together in good faith to wind up the engagement smoothly and collaboratively.
