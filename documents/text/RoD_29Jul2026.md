## 1. Meeting Objective
To discuss areas of convergence between the Directorate of Horticulture, Department of Agriculture, Government of Bihar and Quality Council of India (QCI) for improving the quality, standardisation, and marketability of Bihar's makhana (fox nut), with particular reference to export competitiveness, farmer income enhancement, and the creation of a sustainable quality ecosystem.

## 3. Meeting Notes
**1. Dr. Aishwarya Raj (CEO, NBQP, QCI) provided a briefing on the mandate and organisational structure of QCI for the benefit of all participants:**
QCI is an autonomous body set up by the Ministry of Commerce and Industry, and is the national accreditation body in India, with a larger focus on the healthcare and laboratory sectors. QCI has five boards — NABL, NABCB, NABH, NABET and NABQ — and four operational divisions: Project Planning & Implementation Division (PPID), Project Analysis & Documentation Division, NDIE (Division for Industry Excellence), and the Strategy & Policy Division. QCI does not itself conduct testing or certification; it accredits the bodies and laboratories that perform such functions. QCI is governed by a 21-member Governing Body representing the Government of India, industry, and industry associations, and is headed by a Secretary General, with the Chairperson appointed by the Hon'ble Prime Minister of India. QCI's other programs like Sarpanch Samwaad (outreach to village leaders & platform for sharing best practices between the Sarpanches); QCI's Gunvatta Gurukul initiative – a free eight-week training for 100 students (per batch) nationwide.

**2. Shri Amit Singh (Joint Director, NBQP, QCI) outlined QCI's existing engagement with Bihar:**
Quality Council of India (QCI) has established a dedicated Project Management Unit (PMU) at the Bihar Institute of Public Administration and Rural Development (BIPARD) since Dec 2024 to provide comprehensive technical, engineering, and strategic support. This initiative aims to enhance BIPARD's institutional capacity by assisting in infrastructure planning, project execution, and technical consultations across various engineering domains. The PMU plays a crucial role in streamlining processes, improving operational efficiency, and ensuring high-quality outcomes in project implementation. QCI also provides guidance on best practices and ensures adherence to regulatory and quality standards.
Makhana Gunvatta Manthan: A stakeholder engagement roundtable held on 17 July 2026 with approximately 100+ participants, including government bodies (ICAR, APEDA, NABARD, Makhana Board), industry associations, farmers and processors. More Manthans or such events should be conducted by QCI in collaboration with the DoH, Bihar, to spread awareness and discuss the challenges and solutions to the same.

**3. Shivam (Analyst, NBQP) presented key findings from the Makhana Gunvatta Manthan:**
Market context: Makhana has seen rising global demand, with consumption shifting from occasional to daily use; major export markets are North America, Europe, the Middle East, and Asia-Pacific.
Critical challenges identified: quality issues (inconsistent sizing, poor processing, high moisture content); phytosanitary & packaging concerns leading to high export rejections; non-compliance due to limited awareness among farmers and processors; absence of a robust traceability system; and insufficient NABL-accredited testing infrastructure in Bihar.
Farmer aspirations: Farmers seek to grow into exporters but currently lack the training, awareness, and certifications & process required for export markets.
Four objectives of the initiative: reduce export rejections; increase farmer income; expand market access (interstate and international); and address growing competition from nine other states now cultivating makhana.
The next step should be the official collaboration of QCI & Department of Horticulture, Government of Bihar, in the field of Makhana ecosystem.

**4. Director Horticulture, Bihar (Shri Abhishek Kumar) outlined specific areas where QCI's expertise and support are required:**
- Standardisation of the traditional "sutta" grading system through a precise, measurable, diameter-based definition acceptable in both domestic and export markets; QCI noted that a traditional reference to "sutta" as one-eighth of an inch (~3.17 mm) remains unvalidated, and that Codex has international standards for physical, chemical and microbiological parameters of makhana, though the sutta system itself remains unstandardised.
- Standardisation of moisture-content thresholds (a range of 10–12% currently fetches a good price) to determine pricing tiers and ensure storage viability.
- Development of QCI quality standards to define the conditions under which makhana may be stored and an Electronic Negotiable Warehouse Receipt (e-NWR) issued by the Warehouse Development Regulatory Authority (WDRA), enabling price discovery, credit access (banks are mandated to lend against 70–80% of e-NWR value), and extended shelf life; QCI noted that its existing six-star rating framework for warehouses and cold storage (developed with FCI and NHB, covering 114 parameters and over 4,000 facilities assessed nationally) could be extended to Bihar's makhana supply chain.
- Provision of quality standards to refine and validate the smart storage technology being developed under the signed MoU between the Directorate of Horticulture and IIT Kharagpur for the smart storage system of makhana.
- Standardisation of Modified Atmosphere Packaging (MAP) and other packaging protocols required for export compliance.
- Training of farmers and processors in Good Agricultural Practices (GAP) and Good Handling Practices (GHP), leveraging QCI's pro bono training capacity, with target farmers to be identified through Directorate institutions such as the National Research Centre (NRC) Makhana.
- Strengthening of the technical capacity of the Plant Protection Division, Department of Agriculture, Bihar, which is authorised to issue phytosanitary certificates but remains under-resourced; QCI also noted that APEDA has an office in Bihar, though accessibility to phytosanitary certification remains a bottleneck.
- Establishment of an NABL-accredited laboratory for makhana testing, proposed under National Makhana Board funding with a sanctioned budget of approximately Rs. 7–8 crore for the current financial year; QCI to assist in drafting a standardised RFP/tender document. QCI clarified that NABL cannot itself establish a laboratory owing to conflict of interest with its accreditation role, but its NABET division may be able to assist, subject to further exploration.
- Piloting blockchain technology for supply-chain traceability with a small group of 2–3 existing exporters, funded under National Makhana Board innovation funds, with a phased approach to scale to more processors and FPOs; Dr. Pradeep Hazari (PMU Head), based on a seven-day visit to the Makhana belt, identified three critical areas for QCI intervention: quality/processing (currently manual, firewood-dependent, and largely handled by the Mallah community), testing (dependent on the proposed NABL-accredited laboratory), and traceability/audit (best addressed through blockchain).
- Export data context: Based on APEDA's first year of HS-Code-segregated export data, Bihar's share of national makhana exports is disproportionately low, while Haryana accounts for approximately 70% and Haryana, Maharashtra and Kerala together account for over 94%, largely due to exporters sourcing from Bihar but conducting packing and loading elsewhere; QCI was advised to engage with Haryana-based exporters to understand best practices and gaps.

**5. Additional discussion points were raised:**
- Zero Effect, Zero Defect (ZED) ratings: Several processors attending the Gunvatta Manthan were already aware of the ZED rating process; units in Darbhanga, Katihar, Madhubani and Purnia have received ZED certification under the Ministry of MSME; stakeholders requested awareness programmes to promote the ZED policy model.
- National Makhana Day/Mahotsav: ICAR-NRC Makhana proposed designating 17 October as Makhana Day; the Directorate clarified that Bihar traditionally organises an annual Makhana Mahotsav, typically timed around the GI-tag award date during the July–October harvest period; a proposal is under consideration with the Government of India to designate Sharad Purnima (of cultural relevance to makhana in Mithila, including the Kojaagra ritual) as a Commemorative Day, subject to due approval from the competent authority; QCI's engagements will be integrated into the annual Mahotsav.
- NDIE's ZED certification scheme, applicable to Udyam-registered manufacturing MSMEs, may potentially apply to makhana processing units; QCI will evaluate the applicability and potential systems/process interventions.

**6. Dr. Aishwarya Raj recommended the following strategic approach for the agreed way forward:**
- Prioritise 2–3 immediately achievable ("low-hanging fruit") items rather than attempting all areas simultaneously, targeting visible results within 2–4 months, before proceeding to larger, longer-timeline initiatives.
- Institute a regular review cadence between QCI and the Directorate of Horticulture — fortnightly initially, moving to monthly.
- Build the Directorate's internal capacity to ensure quality systems remain sustainable, and avoid commitments exceeding QCI's current capacity.
- Target an eventual joint public announcement (by the Chief Minister/Governor of Bihar) following 2–4 months of demonstrated progress.
- Immediate priorities identified: (i) standardisation of grading and moisture-content thresholds aligned with Codex and export requirements; (ii) capacity-building/awareness training for farmers, processors and Directorate officials on GAP, GHP and export compliance; and (iii) support for the NABL-accredited laboratory ecosystem (via NABET/NDIE) and a blockchain traceability pilot with 2–3 progressive exporters.
