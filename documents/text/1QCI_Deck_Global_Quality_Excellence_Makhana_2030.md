# Global Quality Excellence in Makhana by 2030 — A 1QCI Approach

## Slide 1 — Title
Global Quality Excellence in Makhana by 2030 — A 1QCI Approach to the National Makhana Board. Presented to the Leadership of the National Makhana Board, September 2026.

## Slide 2 — Context of this presentation: what was asked of us, and what we have done
- **Request:** Letter dated 5 August 2026 from the Director, Horticulture, Government of Bihar, invoking the DA&FW–QCI MoU of 11 December 2024, seeks technical support, accreditation hand-holding and a dedicated technical team across nine areas of makhana quality infrastructure, copied to the Secretary DA&FW and the Nodal Officer, National Makhana Board.
- **Reference:** DA&FW–QCI MoU dated 11 December 2024 (Articles 1 to 5, 11); Central Sector Scheme for Development of Makhana and the Board's Operational Guidelines; Concept Note and Action Framework enclosed with Letter 3064; PIB Backgrounder on the makhana sector, 19 August 2026; QCI scheme-development precedents (CSUAS, Coconut GAP, VCSMPP).
- **Output (subject matter of this deck):** One programme carrying fourteen workstreams across six QCI Boards and Divisions, with a Makhana Quality Scheme as its core; the methodology, sequence, evidence base and delivery model for each workstream; and the decisions required of the Board to convert this into a work programme.

## Slide 3 — As-is: where the sector stands today, on the official record
Figures as published by the Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare — the baseline against which the programme will be measured.
- **80,590 MT** production in 2025–26 (Second Advance Estimates), up from 63,910 MT in 2024–25. Bihar 60,000 MT, about three-fourths of national output and 80–85 percent of global supply.
- **7,264.89 MT** exported in 2025–26, valued at ₹192.96 crore — the first year of product-specific customs data, after DGFT created a separate HSN code in 2025.
- **77%** of export value goes to three markets: United States 40%, Canada 20%, UAE 17%. Germany (USD 26/kg), Nepal (21.6) and Australia (21) pay more than the US (19.5) on small volumes.
- **₹476.03 Cr.** Central Sector Scheme outlay, 2025–26 to 2030–31; ₹30 crore approved for 2025–26 and ₹90 crore for 2026–27; 8,159 farmers benefited in 2025–26.
- Three statements in the same record define where the assurance layer must be built:
  - *Processing is still a household craft* — "Primary processing of makhana is mostly carried out at the household or village level using traditional tools and methods." Consistency has to be built into a documented process standard.
  - *Grade is size, judged by eye* — "After popping, the makhana is polished, sorted, and graded according to size and quality. Larger, higher-quality makhana fetch higher prices." What is missing is a grade a buyer can verify without being in the mandi.
  - *The Board has named the infrastructure* — The Board's first meeting stressed "infrastructure for grading, drying, popping and packaging" and "export readiness". Every workstream attaches to an asset the Board is already funding.
- Source: PIB Backgrounder (19 Aug 2026) Release ID 2301052; PIB, 'National Makhana Board holds first meeting' (12 Dec 2025) Release ID 2203165.

## Slide 4 — Problem Statements
- **Ref 1: What the buyer cannot verify**
  - P1 Grade rests on the "Suta" convention: understood by custom, not defined in measurable parameters, so it cannot be contracted, arbitrated or verified at a distance.
  - P2 The Mithila Makhana GI protects origin but does not certify quality; no third-party mark distinguishes authentic, quality-assured produce.
  - P3 Provenance is asserted, not proven: a paper-based, fragmented chain cannot furnish the chain-of-custody evidence export buyers require.
- **Ref 2: What the unit cannot demonstrate**
  - P4 Processing industrialised within a decade with no settled body of documented practice; critical control points are managed by feel.
  - P5 No accredited testing capacity sits inside the makhana belt; samples travel out at a cost small processors cannot absorb.
  - P6 Most units are micro or small enterprises without a quality management system or a credible, incentive-linked upgrade path.
- **Ref 3: What the system cannot see**
  - P7 No systematic market surveillance exists; adulteration, misgrading and false GI claims go undetected and unredressed.
  - P8 The technical workforce, from graders to laboratory analysts, has had little access to structured quality training.
  - P9 There is no permanent institutional home for quality in the sector and no instrument that measures progress.

## Slide 6 — QCI's proposal for an NMB-led Makhana Quality Framework
- **Aspiration:** Global Quality Excellence in Makhana by 2030
- **Pillar 1 – Standards, Traceability & Field-level Quality:** WS-01 (grading and process standards, certification system, quality mark, traceability and surveillance design); WS-06 periodic quality surveillance report
- **Pillar 2 – Skilling:** WS-10, WS-11 skilling; WS-12 ZED
- **Pillar 3 – Testing:** WS-02 laboratory needs assessment and network design; WS-03 calibration protocol; WS-09 accreditation hand-holding
- **Pillar 4 – Demand Creation & Export Readiness:** WS-05 export-preparedness evaluation; WS-13 seminars, workshops and conferences; WS-14 Export Excellence Awards (to be explored – Buyer Seller Meets)
- **Pillar 5 – Ecosystem Enablers:** WS-02 implementation of network design; WS-04 processing gap assessment
- **Institutional foundation:** WS-07 grievance and non-conformity redressal mechanism | WS-08 PMU / Quality Cell | quality dashboard and annual public sectoral review
- **Mandate:** DA&FW–QCI MoU of 11 December 2024, Article 2: an architecture and roadmap for laboratory capacity, market surveillance and sampling protocols, and training of laboratory personnel

## Slide 7 — Three stages, five gates, thirty-six months
Standards must precede certification, and accreditation readiness must precede laboratory commissioning.
- **Stage 1 – Foundational (Months 0–12):** PMU mobilised; focal points and JWG constituted; integrated baseline (Suta characterisation, unit audits, laboratory inventory); grading and process standards drafted; scopes defined; traceability pilot designed; curriculum built; outreach series launched
- **Stage 2 – Build-out (Months 12–24):** grading standard notified through BIS; scheme approved by SC; first CBs empanelled; pilot certifications; first laboratories accredited under ISO/IEC 17025; ZED cluster drive live; traceability pilot executed; surveillance framework operational
- **Stage 3 – Scale and institutionalise (Months 24–36):** certification and mark at scale; buyer and retailer recognition; accredited laboratory network functional across the belt; Makhana Quality Cell staffed; knowledge transfer complete; Export Excellence Awards instituted; independent impact assessment
- **Five gates:** Gate 1 (Month 3) baseline and diagnostic plan accepted; Gate 2 (Month 9) draft grading specification and scheme Part 3 cleared by TC; Gate 3 (Month 15) scheme approved by SC, grading standard with BIS, first CB provisionally approved; Gate 4 (Month 24) first laboratories accredited, pilot certifications complete, scheme upgraded; Gate 5 (Month 33) Quality Cell operating, impact assessment, decision on MoU extension

## Slides 8–16 — Part Two: WS-01 The Makhana Quality Scheme (PADD)
**Scheme architecture (slide 9):** Government owns, QCI designs and orchestrates, accredited bodies assure.
- *Scheme owner:* National Makhana Board (GI proprietor as co-owner at the Board's option) — adopts the scheme and notifies the grading standard; owns and licenses the mark; chairs the Steering Committee; links certification to incentives and procurement.
- *Scheme concept developer:* QCI–PADD — problem definition; governance (SC, TC, CC, secretariat); standards (grading IS, process, GHP/HACCP tiers); scheme document Parts 1–7, CB criteria, mark rules, fee model; traceability and surveillance design; dashboard architecture.
- *Accreditation ecosystem:* NABCB (product CBs ISO/IEC 17065; inspection bodies 17020; personnel certification 17024) and NABL (belt laboratories 17025; PT linkage 17043); surveillance of CABs.
- *Quality excellence orchestrator:* QCI–PMU (PPID), NBQP, NDIE — Gunvatta Yatra, Samriddhi, Mitra, Pathshala, Manthan; grievance monitoring and market surveillance; ZED ladder; Export Excellence Awards; annual sectoral review.
- Cross-cutting: TCB (WS-09, 10, 11); digital quality infrastructure (traceability ledger, LIMS linkage, QR verification, quality dashboard).

**Design logic (slide 10) — seven components:**
| Problem | Focal question | Component | What it delivers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | On what measurable basis can a grade be written into a contract and verified by a third party? | Grading standard | Suta classes converted into size band, bulk density, moisture, popping recovery, colour index and defect tolerances; draft IS via BIS with ICAR–NRC Makhana (to be explored in parallel); bilingual chart and reference samples |
| P4 | How does every unit make the same product on every shift? | Process standard and GHP/HACCP manual | Documented chain to shelf-life declaration; CCPs; tiered basic / intermediate / export-ready compliance; SOPs and record formats |
| P5 | What makes a Darbhanga test report acceptable to a buyer in Hamburg or Dubai? | Accreditation scope definition | Parameter-by-parameter scope per laboratory: residues vs MRLs, heavy metals, mycotoxins, microbiology, moisture, proximate, physical grading; PT linkage |
| P2 | What can a buyer rely on that a seller cannot simply claim? | Certification scheme and quality mark | Product certification on the two standards; owner and assessor separated; GI authentication and anti-adulteration protocol; tiered fee model |
| P3 | How is origin proven at every node? | Traceability system design | Data schema and mandatory node records pond-to-consignment; verification and audit protocol; QR buyer verification; APEDA compatibility |
| P7 | Who checks the market, on what sampling frame, with what chain of custody? | Market surveillance framework | Surveillance across mandis, units, retail, export; masked and random sampling; laboratory referral; link to grievance mechanism |
| P9 | How does the Board see the system it owns? | Quality dashboard | Indicators: laboratories accredited, units certified, personnel trained, consignments detained, certified vs uncertified price differentials |

**Delivery model (slide 11):** every stage of assurance delivered by an accredited body — grading (ISO/IEC 17065, 17024), processing (17065), testing (17025, 17043; ILAC MRA recognition), GI authenticity (17020), sampling and surveillance (17024), provenance (digital QI, APEDA-compatible ledger).

**Scheme document (slide 12):** Part 1 Introduction and scope (raw guri, popped makhana, value-added products; relationship to GI and FSSAI); Part 2 Governance (SC, TC, CC, secretariat, appeals); Part 3 Technical criteria (grading spec, process standard, GHP/HACCP tiers, testing limits; each clause traceable to IS, ISO, Codex or FSSAI); Part 4 Certification process; Part 5 Requirements for CBs (ISO/IEC 17065 via NABCB plus makhana competence); Part 6 Mark and GI authentication; Part 7 Approval procedure and fees (tiered so FPOs and micro-units are not priced out). Plus annexures (forms, reports, licence agreement).

**Method (slide 13) — eight steps:** 1 Charter and instrument (Wk 0–6); 2 Committees constituted (Wk 4–10); 3 Baseline and characterisation (Mo 2–5); 4 Part 3 drafted (Mo 4–8); 5 Parts 4–7 and formats (Mo 7–10); 6 TC, CC, SC approval (Mo 9–12); 7 Pilot certification with 2–3 units/FPOs (Mo 12–18); 8 Upgrade and hand-over to Makhana Quality Cell (Mo 18–24).

**Governance and sustainability (slide 14):** Steering Committee chaired by NMB; Technical Committee with ICAR–NRC Makhana as scientific anchor; Certification Committee with NABCB; secretariat PADD until hand-over. Mark owned by NMB (or jointly with GI proprietor), issued only on certification plus Mithila origin authentication; QR link. Self-sustaining: tiered fees, group certification for FPOs, ZED/State incentive convergence, risk-based surveillance; recurring elements fee-borne.

**Components and timelines (slide 15):** 1 Grading standard (M0–18); 2 Process standard and GHP/HACCP (M3–18); 3 Accreditation scope definition (M0–12); 4 Certification scheme and mark (M6–24); 5 Traceability design and pilot (M6–24); 6 Market surveillance framework (M12–24); 7 Quality dashboard (M12–36). Timings follow Annexure I of Letter 3064.

**Precedents (slide 16):** CSUAS (DGCA/MoCA), Voluntary Certification Scheme for GAP, VCSMPP (NMPB, Ministry of AYUSH), Conformity Assessment Framework for Digital Competencies (NeGD, MeitY), ZED (Ministry of MSME), crop-protection quality infrastructure (in discussion).

## Slides 17–25 — Part Three: PPID projects (WS-02 to WS-08)
- **WS-02 Laboratory needs assessment and network design** — PPID; Phase A; one-time; Months 0–12. Method: secondary research (labs sanctioned under cluster, horticulture, food-processing, FPO schemes from BHDS/NMB records); district demand mapping (prioritise Darbhanga, Madhubani, Purnia, Katihar, Saharsa, Supaul); stakeholder consultation; scope categorisation (baseline vs reference sites); network design. Deliverables: needs-assessment report; proposed network design; district-wise prioritisation note.
- **WS-03 Calibration protocol for mechanical grading and sorting equipment** — PPID; Phase B; continuous; Months 12–24 then recurring. Method: equipment inventory; reference against grading spec; tolerance and frequency; field validation; field documentation. Deliverables: calibration and verification procedure per equipment type; tolerance ranges and frequency; field checklist.
- **WS-04 Gap assessment and readiness audit of processing units** — PPID; Phase B; Months 6–18 then recurring. Context: 40–42% popping recovery and 30–40% post-production losses under scheme cost norms; four enterprise tiers by raw-seed capacity (micro to 100 kg/day, small 200–500, medium 1,500, large above 1,500). Deliverables: audit report; structured checklist; district-wise upgradation plan.
- **WS-05 Evaluation of exporters and FPOs on export preparedness** — PPID; Phase B; Months 18–30. Deliverables: pilot evaluation report; export-preparedness assessment beyond the pilot cohort; gap assessment ahead of wider onboarding.
- **WS-06 Periodic quality surveillance report** — PPID; Phase B; Months 18–36 recurring. Deliverables: analytical framework and indicator set; recurring report; evidence feed to Board and State decisions.
- **WS-07 Grievance and non-conformity redressal mechanism** — PPID; Phase B; one-time design; Months 12–18. Deliverables: intake channels and taxonomy; end-to-end workflow; linkage and escalation framework; operating protocol for the Quality Cell.
- **WS-08 Makhana Quality PMU** — PPID. Ten-person core: Team Lead (1), Project Manager (1), Associate Manager Technical and Quality (1), Associate Manager Field and Implementation (1), Analyst Data/MIS/Monitoring (1), Analyst Technical and Documentation (1), Project Coordinators (4). All except Team Lead deployed at client site. Approach: mobilisation → diagnostic and baseline planning → common frameworks → specialist mobilisation → field implementation → consolidation → integrated recommendations → continuous monitoring → knowledge management. Integrated baseline covers twelve areas. Deliverables include mobilisation plan, baseline plan, integrated workplan, trackers, risk register, monthly/quarterly reports, central MIS and dashboard, institutionalisation report.

## Slides 26–29 — Part Four: TCB projects (WS-09 to WS-11)
- **WS-09 Laboratory accreditation hand-holding** — TCB (NABET) with NABL; Phase A then C; Months 0–24, Mitra support from Month 24. Method: design-stage review; quality system; method validation and PT; personnel competence; pre-assessment and application; post-development Mitra support. Precedent: ICMR 60-laboratory and FSSAI/AGMARK 12-laboratory programmes.
- **WS-10 Tiered curriculum and master trainer programme** — TCB; Phase B; Months 3–18, delivery continues. Four tiers: awareness, operational (GHP, GMP, HACCP), technical, management. Bilingual material on Gunvatta Pathshala; master trainers from KVKs, SAUs, ICAR–NRC, BHDS, FPO federations.
- **WS-11 Inspector training and certification-linked skilling** — TCB; Phase B then C; Months 12–36 recurring. Role definition; course design (sampling frames, chain of custody, evidentiary standards, Acts and Rules); delivery; credentialing (pathway to ISO/IEC 17024); annual refresher.

## Slides 30–31 — Part Five: NDIE (WS-12)
- **WS-12 ZED in cluster mode** — NDIE; Phase B then C; Months 6–30, progression tracking to Month 36. Cluster mobilisation with DICs, BHDS, FPO federations; cluster-mode assessment; hand-holding aligned to WS-01 tiers; incentive convergence with MSME and State schemes; bronze → silver → gold progression and recognition.

## Slides 32–36 — Part Six: NBQP outreach and recognition (WS-13, WS-14) — proposed by QCI in addition to the nine areas requested
- **WS-13 Seminars, workshops and conferences at four levels** — NBQP; Months 0–36. International (3 days, ~300), National (2 days, ~300/day), State (2 days, ~250/day), District (2 days, ~200). Knowledge cascade from international to district, with grassroots feedback flowing back. NBQP track record: Gunvatta Sankalp, Gunvatta Samarpan, Gunvatta Mahotsav, Gunvatta Yatra and Manthan, Regional Assessor Conclaves, National Quality Conclave (with DPIIT, Feb 2026).
- **WS-14 Export Excellence Awards** — on the Coconut Development Board model. Categories: Best Makhana Exporter (Gold/Silver/Bronze); Best Value-Added Product Exporter; Best Emerging or Market-Expansion Exporter; Best Woman Makhana Exporter; Best Innovative Exporter; Best FPO Exporter. Weights (indicative): export performance 35%, market reach 15%, value addition 15%, quality/compliance 15%, innovation 10%, farmer and ecosystem impact 10%. Six-month cycle from framework to ceremony.
