1. **Title** — Makhana: From Indian Fields to Global Tables. India's ancient superfood takes center stage in the global health food revolution.
2. **Driving Quality Excellence for Viksit Bharat 2047**
3. **QCI** — An autonomous body set up by Government of India under the Ministry of Commerce and Industry; established as a national accreditation body through a Cabinet decision in 1996; 1500+ in-house young professionals; 250+ accredited conformity bodies; 10K+ on-ground assessors. Quality Bharat, Viksit Bharat.
4. **QCI's Legacy** (2026)
5. **QCI's Boards & Divisions** — Governing Council (39 members) and Governing Body (21 members), headed by Chairperson QCI; Secretary-General. Accreditation boards: NABL (14,000+ accreditations), NABET (8,000+ schools), NABH (27,000+ accreditations, certifications and empanelment), NABCB (250+ CBs & IBs; 25,000+ MS certificates). Quality promotion: NBQP (50,000+ impacted). Special projects divisions: PADD (voluntary certification schemes — drones, AYUSH, cybersecurity, agriculture); ZED (5,69,000+ MSMEs certified; LEAN, SAMAR); PPID (300+ projects across 50+ ministries).
6. **The Makhana Journey** — Ancient heritage (3,000+ years); modern transformation (pond cultivation to modern processing); global expansion.
7. **India: The Makhana Heartland** — 90% global production (India); 90% Bihar's share of India's production; 40% cultivation growth 2020–2025 (25K → 40K ha); seed production nearly doubled 35K MT → 63K MT (2020–2025). ICAR-NRCM, Darbhanga — backbone of seed improvement, scientific farming, mechanisation.
8. **The Complete Makhana Value Chain** — Cultivation (pond 1,200–1,500 kg/ha; field-based 2,000 kg/ha; 40,000–50,000 MT popped annually); harvesting (Aug–Oct, ~2-month window, 12–20 kg/day/harvester); processing (skilled 40–55% popping yield, unskilled ~30%; only 100–200 mechanical units vs requirement of 5,000); grading (Grade A, Grade B, Chipta; 1 Suta = 3.17 mm; 7 Suta 21 mm+, ₹1,500–1,700/kg); value addition (flavoured snack segment ~35–40% CAGR since 2020); packaging (MAP, nitrogen flushing; hygroscopic product; destination labelling); distribution and export (~25,130 MT in 2024–25, ~4X from 6,700 MT in 2020, 39% CAGR).
9. **Largest Manufacturers & Exporters** — Shakti Sudha Industries (Patna/Darbhanga); Sudha Makhana Bhandar (Darbhanga); Mithila Naturals (Darbhanga); Maa Kamakhya Makhana Industries (North Bihar); Rajendra Makhana Udyog (Darbhanga); Shree Murli Manohar Makhana (Purnia).
10. **Comparative Analysis of Makhana Production Across States** — note: Delhi/NCR and Mumbai/Gujarat capture 18–20% of export stage and 20% of marketing despite zero cultivation.

12. **Export Opportunities & Challenges** — export markets require documented purity ≥99%, moisture ≤7%, size in mm (Sutta labelling alone not accepted). Key markets: US 40%, Canada 20%; Europe 27%; Middle East 10%; Asia-Pacific 3%.
13. **Elevating Makhana to Global Standards** — potential; quality focus; strategic interventions; global leadership.
14. **Critical Challenge: Export Rejections** — quality issues, phytosanitary concerns, non-compliance, traceability gaps. Core pattern: limited pre-shipment testing in Darbhanga & Madhubani; no formal product standard translating Indian grading into verifiable parameters; packaging quality. Only 2% of produce exported.
15. **Reducing Export Rejections** — GAP/GHP; processing SOPs; MAP and nitrogen flushing; blockchain traceability; phytosanitary certification via accredited labs; farmer training.
16. **Legal & Regulatory Requirements for Export** — APEDA registration; IEC; FSSAI licence; country-specific regulations (FDA, EFSA, CFIA); labelling; phytosanitary certificates; GI tag advantage; organic/Kosher/Halal certifications.
